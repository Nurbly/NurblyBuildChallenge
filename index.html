Is this for you?

Who it's for

Engineering students, hobbyists and working CAD designers. Enter solo or as a team. You need to be 18 or older.

What you'll make

One complete drone, one subsystem a month, in a public repo that shows every revision. It's a portfolio piece you can link to.

What you need

Any CAD tool that exports STEP and a free Nurbly account. No hardware, no budget, no Git knowledge.

What you get

  • Written feedback from the judges after every stage.
  • A chance at a cash prize every month, not just at the end.
  • A public design history to show employers and clients.
  • Other teams fork and remix your design in the final stage.
  • Your design stays yours. You keep the IP.

Keeping the IP means you own your design, but your entry is open source. You choose an open license, such as CERN-OHL or CC BY-SA, and anyone can view, fork, build and reuse your design under its terms. Nurbly and its sponsors may also show your work to promote the challenge. Section 6 of the rules has the details.

How to enter

Step 1

Make a free account at nurbly.com.

Step 2

Register your team. One form per team, filled in by the team lead.

Step 3

Create a public repo with nbc-2027 in its name or description. Add an open source license.

Step 4

Each month, upload your STEP files and notes on your decisions. Tag the version you're submitting stage-N before the deadline.

Registering early? Stage 1 opens on 1 Mar 2027. Stage briefs, deadlines and results go to the team lead's email. Until then you can set up your repo and join our Discord to ask questions and talk with other entrants.

Four words you'll see. A commit is a saved version of your files. A tag marks the version you're submitting. A fork is your own copy of another team's repo, and a pull request offers your changes back to them.

Stages

One stage per month, each building on the last. Every deadline is the last Sunday of its month in 2027, at 23:59 UTC.

StageDeadlineDeliverable
stage-1 Thesis and airframe28 MarA thesis stating what the drone is for and the requirements that follow. Arms, body, landing gear and motor mounts as STEP. Printable or machinable.
stage-2 Electronics layout25 AprFlight controller, ESC and battery mounts, cable routing. PCB outline if custom.
stage-3 Propulsion30 MayMotor selection and prop guards. Thrust math documented in the repo.
stage-4 Control and sensing27 JunFC mount, camera or sensor gimbal, antenna placement.
stage-5 Integration25 JulFull assembly, interference check, mass budget, BOM.
stage-6 Release and remix29 AugA v1.0 release with build guide, BOM and drawings. Remix another team's entry for up to 50 of the stage's 100 points. Fork it, change one subsystem, and open a pull request. You can submit your release without a remix.

How it is judged

  • Engineering quality35
  • Design process20
  • Integration with prior stages20
  • Documentation15
  • Community interest10

Stages 1 to 5 are scored out of 100 as above. Stage 6 scores your release (50) and your remix of another team's entry (50). The remix is optional, but skipping it gives up those 50 points. Every stage also offers up to 10 bonus points for merged pull requests from outside contributors, and up to 5 for a physical part.

Community interest counts the stars and forks on your repo at each deadline. A fork counts as two stars, and the total is scored relative to all entries.

There is no minimum commit count. Judges read your whole history, and recorded decisions and revisions that improve the design score higher than one finished upload on deadline day.

FAQ

Do I need to build the drone physically?

No. A physical build is optional. Each stage offers a bonus of up to 5 points for a photo of a physical part of that stage's subsystem, at any scale.

What CAD tool do I need?

Any tool that exports STEP. Nurbly shows STEP files in 3D right in the browser and compares revisions face by face, so judges can see how your design changed. Native files, like SolidWorks parts, can live in the repo too. Only STEP gets the 3D view.

Do I need to know Git?

No. Nurbly runs on Git, but you never type a git command. Upload files in the browser, or use the free nrb command line tool and desktop companion, which offers to check in changes from your system tray.

What does Nurbly cost?

Nothing. Challenge repos are public, and public repos on Nurbly are free with no limit on collaborators. A team of any size can compete on free accounts.

How big can our repo be?

A free Nurbly repo holds up to 500 MiB. That is plenty for STEP files. Keep build photos and videos compressed.

Can I join late?

Yes. You can join from any stage until registration closes on 25 Jul 2027. A late team can win the stage prize for any stage it submits, and the grand prize if it submits stages 4, 5 and 6.
